# Line Maintenance Manual (sample, fictional)

## §8.4 CNC spindle bearing inspection (p.212)
Applies to CNC-07 and other 5-axis machining centres. Rising spindle vibration above 4.5 mm/s RMS, a grinding or rumbling noise, or bearing temperature above 70 °C indicate bearing wear or lubrication failure. Stop the spindle, apply lockout/tagout, and inspect the bearing grease condition. Measure axial play with a dial gauge; above 0.02 mm the bearing must be replaced. Do not run production batches until the inspection is signed off.

## §8.7 Spindle lubrication schedule (p.218)
Grease the spindle bearings every 500 operating hours using the approved high-speed grease. Over-greasing raises bearing temperature; under-greasing raises vibration. Record each lubrication in the CMMS.

## §5.2 Hydraulic press oil temperature (p.131)
Applies to Press-02. Normal hydraulic oil temperature is 40–55 °C. Above 60 °C, check the oil level, cooler fan and heat exchanger. Repeated high temperature after topping up usually indicates an internal leak at the main cylinder seal. A leaking seal is a pressure-system safety issue: report to the shift supervisor and plan seal replacement.

## §5.6 Main cylinder seal replacement (p.140)
Seal replacement requires depressurising the hydraulic circuit, lockout/tagout, and two qualified technicians. Estimated duration: 3 hours. Keep a spare seal kit in stores; reorder when stock falls below one.

## §3.1 Conveyor belt tension (p.64)
Applies to Conveyor-C. Belt slip, tracking drift or motor current fluctuations indicate incorrect tension. Re-tension using the take-up screws until deflection is 10–15 mm at mid-span. Close the related work order after a 15-minute test run.

## §3.4 Conveyor drive motor overload (p.70)
Motor current above 110% of rated current for more than 5 minutes indicates jamming, a seized roller or a failing gearbox. Stop the conveyor, clear the jam and check rollers by hand rotation with power isolated.

## §11.2 Robot R4 joint motor current anomalies (p.305)
Applies to Robot-R4. Sudden current spikes on a joint motor usually indicate a collision, a payload outside specification, or a failing reducer. Check the collision log first. If spikes repeat without collisions, inspect the joint reducer for backlash and schedule a reducer check within 48 hours.

## §11.5 Robot cell guarding and safety interlocks (p.312)
Never bypass the cell door interlock. If an interlock fault occurs, the robot must not be restarted until a supervisor has verified the guarding. Any guarding defect is a safety-critical item.
